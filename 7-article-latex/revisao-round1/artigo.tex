\begin{abstract}
Vehicular edge nodes run monitoring models continuously on hardware that
cannot compute every frame at full precision. Selective computation---skipping
or reusing inference steps---keeps such systems within energy and latency
budgets, but it is safe only if the model's uncertainty rises near critical
transitions. In continuous driving streams this is not guaranteed: a model
trained only for classification can remain confident up to a critical
transition, so a confidence-driven scheduler may suppress exactly the frames on
which detection depends. We argue that what renders uncertainty usable for
scheduling is not calibration but its \emph{temporal organization}---whether
the signal separates stable intervals from the moments around risk onset. We
study this through the TERA ecosystem: \mbox{TERA-Gen} generates episodes with
deterministic, frame-precise onsets; \mbox{AF-TOI} induces temporal organization
offline by aligning a compact causal model to an onset-oriented reference; and
\mbox{MHEG} exploits the resulting entropy stratification at runtime. In a
$2\times2$ factorial design over twelve seeds, adding the scheduler to an
unorganized model leaves the miss rate unchanged yet worsens the effective
detection delay ($\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$) by 13.9\% ($p=0.001$, exact
paired Wilcoxon), while on \mbox{AF-TOI}-organized trajectories the same
scheduler achieves \CostReduction\% cost reduction
($\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ 0.099\,s vs.\ 0.570\,s). A post-hoc calibration
control shows that probabilistic calibration and temporally conditioned
reliability vary independently. The pipeline emits a discrete operational-risk
state $\ell_t$ at $O(1)$ cost, a candidate input for vehicular edge
coordination.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introducao}
Consider a monitoring model running on a vehicle's on-board unit. It receives a
multimodal sensor stream and must flag a dangerous condition before it escalates,
frame after frame, on a low-power microcontroller or automotive-grade accelerator
that has no opportunity to offload the computation to remote infrastructure while
the event is unfolding~\cite{bouhsissin2023driver,ortega2020dmd}. Each frame has to
be processed inside a latency budget fixed jointly by the stream's frame rate and
the device's energy and thermal envelope; embedded driver-monitoring systems show
that real-time inference within such a budget is achievable on automotive-grade
hardware~\cite{hariharan2023driveredge,tinydl2025driverstate}. Two consequences
follow. First, the operative constraint is not accuracy in isolation but the
ability to keep detecting critical events while the node manages its energy and
compute over uninterrupted operation. Second, in vehicular and ad hoc edge
networks what the node emits should itself be compact and timely enough to feed
downstream coordination. Driver behavior monitoring is a representative instance of
this regime, and the one we use throughout. We treat the per-frame budget as given
and ask a narrower question: what must be true of the model's uncertainty over time
for a scheduler to spend computation selectively within that budget without putting
detection at risk?
Selective computation, the runtime branch of adaptive
computation~\cite{han2022dynamic}, keeps edge inference inside its resource bounds
by conditioning each step on the model's current confidence. SkipRNN~\cite{campos2018skip},
AdaFrame~\cite{wu2019adaframe}, ACT~\cite{graves2016adaptive}, and
LiteEval~\cite{wu2019liteeval} report large efficiency gains from this idea, all
resting on the same premise: that low uncertainty marks a stable, redundant stretch
of input that can be safely skipped. At the edge there is a second requirement --
the node's output has to remain useful beyond its own inference loop, since
resource-aware monitoring can feed alert dissemination, cooperative perception, and
network-level policies -- but the premise about confidence is what the scheduler's
safety turns on.
The premise is usually left implicit: that confidence is a dependable proxy for
computational urgency. It is a reasonable one in activity recognition, where a
model's uncertainty tends to climb as a new state begins. Continuous vehicular
streaming does not guarantee it. A model trained only to separate normal from
critical, with no reference to \emph{when} risk begins, has no incentive to make its
uncertainty behave differently in the seconds before onset than during ordinary
driving. Its uncertainty trajectory can stay flat across the transition. A scheduler
reading that flat signal then treats an emerging-risk frame like any redundant one
and may suppress the recurrent update just as the situation turns, allocating computation away from the critical transition window, at the cost of a missed or late detection. This is the operational risk we are concerned with: not a slightly
higher average latency, but the loss of the specific frames on which a critical
event is caught.
Standard calibration does not detect this failure mode. Probabilistic
calibration~\cite{guo2017calibration} checks that declared confidence matches
empirical accuracy \emph{on average}, pooled over all timesteps; it says nothing
about where a given timestep sits within an episode. A model can therefore be
well calibrated in this marginal sense and still be unsafe to schedule on, because
marginal agreement between confidence and accuracy is silent about the ordering of
uncertainty in time -- and it is precisely that temporal ordering, near onset, that
a causal scheduler consumes. Calibrating the output probabilities cannot supply
information the trajectory never encoded.
We call the property that a scheduler actually needs \emph{temporally conditioned
operational reliability}: the ability of the uncertainty signal to distinguish
transition regions from stable ones at the moment the scheduler acts. Neither
standard calibration nor existing analyses of non-stationary entropy
dynamics~\cite{braverman2020calib,dera2023trust} treat this as a prerequisite for
scheduling. It is also the property that would let a node summarize its situation
for higher-level network functions without shipping raw frames or full predictive
trajectories.
The causal constraint sharpens the problem. On-board inference is strictly causal:
a decision at time $t$ may use only the history up to $t$, so any failure to
separate a transition from a preceding stable interval shows up immediately as an
unsafe suppression, with no future frame to recover from it. And the episodes are
temporally heterogeneous -- the same stream mixes gradual, multi-second build-ups
with abrupt onsets under $600$\,ms~\cite{dingus2016shrp2,bouhsissin2023driver}. On
naturalistic data these differences are hard to see at all: an onset annotated to
within $\approx$0.31\,s at 9.6\,fps blurs scheduler behavior below that scale,
especially when a missed episode carries no explicit
penalty~\cite{dingus2016shrp2,fang2022dada,ortega2020dmd}.
Our hypothesis (Figure~\ref{fig:tera_hypothesis}) is that this cluster of
difficulties is addressed less by tuning the scheduler's thresholds than by
deliberately shaping the model's predictive trajectories during offline training so
that they differentiate in time. Once the trajectories carry that structure, the model's own uncertainty becomes informative about the proximity of a critical event, and the runtime allocator receives an uncertainty signal with operational meaning. On this reading, the apparent conflict
between efficiency and coverage is relieved by reorganizing the model's internal
dynamics, not by threshold engineering -- and the same organized signal is what lets
the node report a discrete operational state rather than a bare, local confidence
value.
{\color{\revcolor}By an \emph{informative scheduling signal} we mean an uncertainty
trajectory whose level separates episode phases: low and stable during
redundant intervals, rising ahead of and during risk transitions. This is the
property depicted in the central block of Figure~\ref{fig:tera_hypothesis}:
without it, the gate has no temporal basis on which to discriminate; with it,
thresholding entropy becomes a meaningful allocation criterion.}
The paper contributes three components that together isolate and exploit this
property. \textit{TERA-Gen} {\color{\revcolor}(the \emph{TERA episode Generator}, the synthetic-data component of the ecosystem)} generates synthetic episodes with a deterministic
onset calibrated against SHRP2 odds ratios~\cite{dingus2016shrp2}; by fixing the
onset instant it removes the annotation variability that would otherwise make
frame-precise induction and evaluation impossible. \textit{AF-TOI} is an
onset-oriented induction protocol that shapes the causal model's predictive
dynamics so they separate stable regions from transition windows, giving the
scheduler an uncertainty signal that means something in time. \textit{MHEG} is
the runtime scheduler that consumes that signal; it pairs an unconditional
kinematic trigger, which forces computation on abrupt physical change, with an
entropic gate whose usefulness depends on the induced organization -- and whose
behavior when that organization is \emph{absent} is measured directly through a
negative control rather than assumed. Beyond choosing between a full recurrent
update and state reuse, \mbox{MHEG} emits a discrete operational-risk state
$\ell_t \in \{\text{Normal},\, \text{Attention},\, \text{Alert},\, \text{Critical}\}$
that is low-bandwidth, interpretable, and produced causally. The three components
constitute the \textit{TERA} (\textit{Temporal Evaluation and Risk Assessment})
ecosystem, in which organization is induced offline and allocation happens online
on the device.
The experiments bear the hypothesis out, and the most telling result is the
negative one. On unorganized trajectories, adding the scheduler leaves the miss
rate untouched but worsens $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ by
{\color{\revcolor}13.9\%}: the scheduler
is active, yet without the enabling temporal property it allocates blindly. Give
the same scheduler \mbox{AF-TOI}-organized trajectories and it cuts effective cost by
\CostReduction\%, with $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ of
{\color{\revcolor}0.099\,s against 0.570\,s}.
A post-hoc calibration control then rules out the obvious alternative explanation:
rescaling probabilities improves calibration but does not reorganize entropy in
time, so the two properties are empirically distinct. \mbox{MHEG} thus works both as a
local selective-computation mechanism and as a source of a compact operational
state that could later connect embedded inference to resource-aware coordination.

Section~\ref{sec:avaliacao} reports the experimental evaluation, including the

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:relacionados}
\subsection{Resource-Constrained Edge AI in Vehicular Edge Computing}
\label{sec:rel_vec}
Vehicular Edge Computing (VEC) and Mobile Edge Computing (MEC) relocate inference
from the cloud to on-board or roadside units, trading round-trip latency for tight
local budgets in energy, memory, and bandwidth~\cite{gill2025edgeai,bouhsissin2023driver,ortega2020dmd}.
The motivation is concrete: embedded automotive platforms such as the STM32H7,
Cortex-M55, and SA8155P cannot run always-on full-precision inference over a
continuous multimodal stream without breaching thermal or energy limits, so some
form of runtime allocation is unavoidable. Edge-native ITS work has answered mostly
on the model side -- quantization~\cite{hariharan2023driveredge} and resource-aware
architecture~\cite{almodhwahi2025facial} -- while the broader VEC and MEC literature
works at the network side, on static compression or task placement across nodes.
Both leave a complementary question comparatively untouched in safety-critical
settings: \emph{when}, within a single continuous on-device stream, is it worth
spending a full inference step (Section~\ref{sec:rel_edgeai})? That single-node
question is the one we take up first, because it has to be answered before
uncertainty can be relied upon as a coordination signal.
{\color{\revcolor}A clarification on scope is warranted: vehicular networks (e.g.,
IEEE 802.11p/DSRC, C-V2X) and general-purpose mobile ad hoc networks differ in
protocol stack, mobility model, and infrastructure assumptions, and we do not
treat them as interchangeable. TERA operates strictly at the computation layer
of a single node -- deciding \emph{when} to spend an inference step -- and is
agnostic to the underlying communication protocol; the operational-risk state
$\ell_t$ it emits is a compact payload that either class of network could
disseminate. Protocol-level concerns such as medium access, routing, and
dissemination latency are outside the scope of this paper.}
\subsection{Trustworthy and Validated Edge AI}
\label{sec:rel_trust}
Efficiency alone is insufficient at the edge; dependability is an equally important requirement.
{\color{\revcolor}Throughout this paper, ``trustworthy'' is used in a restricted,
operational sense: a selective-computation policy is trustworthy when it
preserves critical-episode coverage ($\mathrm{FR} \le 0.05$) while suppressing
computation, under the controlled conditions evaluated here. We make no claim
regarding broader dimensions of trustworthiness (security, privacy, adversarial
robustness), which are out of scope (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).}
Wang et al.~\cite{wang2025trustworthy} argue that resource limits, heterogeneous
environments, and diverse requirements jointly determine whether an edge system can
be trusted, and ML-safety work on uncertainty quantification for autonomous vehicles
makes the same point from the modeling side~\cite{mcallister2017concrete,mohseni2020mlsafety,hendrycks2022mlsafety}.
The recurring lesson is that dependability has to be demonstrated under deployment
conditions, not read off benchmark accuracy. A scheduler that suppresses inference
is squarely in this reliability-critical category, since its skip decisions decide
whether a safety-relevant event is still caught once computation is cut. What
validating such a scheduler requires, concretely, is evidence that the uncertainty
signal it reads actually separates transition regions from stable ones -- and a direct way to establish the role of this property is to remove it experimentally, which is the function of our negative control. To the best of our knowledge, dependability at this level, the
internal structure of the trajectory the scheduler consumes, has not been examined
as a validation target in the trustworthy edge AI literature.
\subsection{Temporal Risk Detection in Continuous Driver Monitoring}
\label{sec:rel_dms}
Automated driver behavior monitoring in naturalistic environments is well
established. The DMD dataset~\cite{ortega2020dmd} provides dense annotations
for seven behavior classes with front-camera and physiological fusion, and
DADA~\cite{fang2022dada} extends coverage to eye tracking in pre-collision
scenarios. Both, however, define onset by retrospective human annotation with
variability of $\approx$0.31\,s at 9.6\,fps.
That imprecision directly affects edge resource-allocation design. Undetected
episodes vanish from latency averages without penalty, and temporal granularity
is too coarse to separate transition zones from stable periods. Without a
deterministically anchored onset, one cannot tell whether the scheduler
invested resources at emergence windows rather than redundant intervals, which
is the central decision the edge system must make.
The SHRP2 study~\cite{dingus2016shrp2} documents temporally heterogeneous risk trajectories (multi-second gradual transitions down to sub-0.6\,s abrupt onsets) with up to twelvefold collision risk~\cite{bouhsissin2023driver}; dominant validation protocols rely on aggregate recognition metrics and evaluate neither onset timing nor scheduler cost differentiation.
A more recent group of systems settles the feasibility question on the hardware we
care about. Salakapuri et al.~\cite{salakapuri2025integrated} run an integrated
distraction and road-object detector on an automotive-grade platform at real-time
rates, and lightweight time-series and TinyML models report the same under tight
budgets~\cite{fresta2025cognitive,tinydl2025driverstate}; our own Safe-Drive system
pairs behavior detection with seatbelt segmentation for per-frame
monitoring~\cite{albarello2025safedrive}. On-device viability has therefore been empirically demonstrated. What these evaluations report, though, is aggregate recognition accuracy and
latency -- not when a risk is detected relative to its onset, and not what it costs
to suppress computation during the transition that leads up to a critical event. The metrics required to expose allocation errors at the scheduler level are therefore not reported. The combination that would expose them -- a deterministic onset, an explicit
penalty for a missed episode, and suppression treated as a resource-allocation
choice -- is what \TERAGen and $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ are designed to supply.
\subsection{Adaptive Inference and Selective Computation in Embedded Systems}
\label{sec:rel_edgeai}
Adaptive computation families such as dynamic networks~\cite{han2022dynamic,montello2025dynamic}, conditional computation and early exiting~\cite{scardapane2024conditional,rahmath2024earlyexit,teerapittayanon2016branchynet,laskaridis2021adaptive}, and split computing~\cite{matsubara2022split,laskaridis2020hapi} cut inference cost by adapting to the input. Two strategies dominate: structural compression (quantization, pruning~\cite{hariharan2023driveredge}) and confidence-based scheduling (SkipRNN~\cite{campos2018skip}, AdaFrame~\cite{wu2019adaframe}, ACT~\cite{graves2016adaptive}, LiteEval~\cite{wu2019liteeval}, selective classification~\cite{geifman2017selective}). Kaya et al.~\cite{kaya2019shallow} show that overthinking, where computation continues past correct classification, stems from reliance on instantaneous confidence rather than trajectory structure. The present work sits in the on-device branch, where the decision is not \emph{where} to run inference but \emph{when} to invest it within a continuous stream.
These efficiency gains are real, but they rest on the assumption that high
confidence signals absent risk and therefore safe suppression. Recent surveys
confirm that output confidence and entropy remain the dominant exit or skip
criteria~\cite{rahmath2024earlyexit}. The assumption holds in activity
recognition, where predictive trajectories exhibit uncertainty sensitive to
state transitions, giving the scheduler a reliable allocation signal.
In vehicular monitoring the assumption breaks down. A model may stay confident
just before onset not because the situation is stable but because its internal
trajectories lack the temporal differentiation that would distinguish
transition zones from preceding stable periods. A scheduler designed for
activity recognition may then invest in redundant timesteps while suppressing
inference at emergence windows, producing an unsafely optimistic efficiency
gain. Current design criteria evaluate scheduling through throughput, latency,
and deployment metrics without treating temporal detection coverage as an
explicit safety constraint. Whether entropy-driven suppression is safe when a
missed critical episode carries a real cost is therefore left open, and it is
exactly the case \AFTOI and \MHEG are built to address.
\subsection{Uncertainty Calibration and Operational Reliability}
\label{sec:rel_uncertainty}
Trustworthy selective computation needs uncertainty that differentiates transition regions, not just uncertainty that matches aggregate frequencies. Predictive uncertainty estimation is established~\cite{gal2016dropout,lakshminarayanan2017simple,hein2019relu}, and probabilistic calibration, the alignment between declared confidence and empirical accuracy~\cite{guo2017calibration}, is an accepted automotive-safety criterion~\cite{tang2022uv,michelmore2020uq}, with Minderer et al.~\cite{minderer2021revisiting} showing it does not improve monotonically with architecture. Calibration, though, is marginal: a perfectly calibrated model may still schedule computation unsafely if trajectories do not differentiate uncertainty at transitions. Braverman et al.~\cite{braverman2020calib} and TRUST~\cite{dera2023trust} confirm the non-stationary character of entropy dynamics without formalizing near-onset behavior as a scheduling signal.
A related question, which the reviewed literature does not seem to have addressed
directly, is whether improving global calibration also changes how uncertainty is
organized in time. There is a structural reason to expect it does not. Post-hoc
techniques -- temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration}, Platt
scaling~\cite{platt1999probabilistic}, isotonic
calibration~\cite{zadrozny2002isotonic} -- rescale the output probabilities without
touching the weights, and therefore without touching the internal trajectory that a
causal scheduler reads. A monotonic rescaling can align aggregate confidence with
accuracy while leaving the ordering of entropy through an episode exactly as it was,
so entropic dynamics that were not discriminative before remain so afterward. We are
not aware of a study in the adaptive causal scheduling literature that tests this
divergence experimentally, and we do so here as an explicit control.
\subsection{Temporal Structure Transfer and Sequential Modeling}
\label{sec:rel_temporal}
Temporal structure transfer~\cite{hinton2015distilling,bajestani2020tkd,zheng2024tempdistiller} and online sequential architectures~\cite{zhao2022testra,lstr2021} advance efficiency and accuracy, but none asks whether the transferred structure enables trustworthy runtime allocation. Conventional transfer objectives spread the alignment signal uniformly, pushing compact models to reproduce average predictive behavior rather than uncertainty dynamics that separate stable from transition regions, so entropy stratification for trustworthy selective computation stays unaddressed. Prior works also target segmented offline clips where the full sequence is available; causal streaming invalidates sequence-level normalization and global attention, so organizational gains from those contexts do not carry over automatically. Explicit induction mechanisms are required.
{\color{\revcolor}The methodological distinction of \mbox{AF-TOI} relative to these
families can be stated explicitly. Temporal knowledge distillation and
sequence-level transfer optimize agreement with a teacher's
\emph{predictions}, spreading the alignment signal uniformly over time; their
objective is accuracy or compression. \mbox{AF-TOI} instead optimizes the
\emph{shape of the uncertainty trajectory} around a deterministic onset,
through asymmetric temporal weighting (Eq.~\ref{eq:ponderacao_aftoi}) anchored
to $t_0$: the training target is entropy stratification across episode phases,
not fidelity to teacher outputs. Uncertainty-aware inference methods consume
uncertainty at runtime but do not reshape its temporal structure during
training, and adaptive/selective computation methods assume an informative
confidence signal rather than inducing one. To the best of our knowledge,
using onset-anchored structural alignment to \emph{create} the scheduling
signal that a selective-computation gate later consumes is the specific
contribution of \mbox{AF-TOI}.}
\subsection{Gaps and Positioning}
\label{sec:rel_gap}
Read across these bodies of work, the same gap recurs, and Table~\ref{tab:panorama}
localizes it in the \textbf{Temp.~Org.} and \textbf{$\Delta H^{\ast}$} columns. Driver
monitoring established the operational stakes but provides neither a
deterministic onset nor an explicit miss penalty, so the cost of suppressing
computation at a transition window cannot be measured. The adaptive inference
literature delivered the efficiency but inherited the premise that high confidence
means absent risk -- a premise that fails once the internal trajectory is
temporally flat. Studies of temporal entropy
dynamics~\cite{braverman2020calib,dera2023trust}
confirmed that entropy is non-stationary but stopped short of treating onset
dynamics as a scheduling property. Temporal
structure transfer built compact models without asking whether the transferred
structure was the kind a scheduler could safely allocate on. In the table, the
\textbf{$\Delta H^{\ast}$} column is \ding{55} for every prior entry and
\ding{51} only for TERA, and \textbf{Temp.~Org.} is uniformly \ding{55} or~$\circ$
across prior work. On the evidence we surveyed, we are not aware of prior work that
uses temporal entropy dynamics near a deterministic onset as a scheduling signal
under an explicit miss-coverage constraint, or that treats temporal organization as
a \emph{necessary} condition along the chain
\emph{Entropy Stratification $\rightarrow$ Informative Scheduling Signal $\rightarrow$
Trustworthy Selective Computation}.
These gaps form a dependency chain. Onset dynamics cannot be characterized
without deterministic annotation; entropy does not gain discriminative power at
transitions without onset-aware training; schedulers cannot allocate safely
without informative entropy. TERA treats temporal organization as the
structural precondition for this chain: \AFTOI promotes the trajectory
structure required for entropy stratification, and \MHEG exploits the resulting
signal to allocate computation selectively and safely at the edge.

\section{From Temporal Organization to Trustworthy Selective Computation}
\label{sec:framework}
%%============================================================
Embedded risk detection processes signals sequentially without access to future
observations, and this causal constraint governs TERA. The on-board model
operates strictly causally at deployment, while coverage stability comes from
the temporal organization induced offline and then exported to the device.
The pipeline ingests a continuous stream $\mathbf{x} = (x_1, x_2, \ldots)$,
where each $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^D$ encodes visual, kinematic, contextual, and
acoustic observations. The output $y_t$ depends only on the history $\{x_1,
\ldots, x_t\}$, which distinguishes the embedded model from bidirectional
architectures. The predictive core is a two-layer unidirectional LSTM with
per-step cost $\mathcal{O}(4(DH{+}H^2))$, compatible with automotive-grade edge
budgets.
{\color{\revcolor}The sensor stream at the input of Figure~\ref{fig:tera_operational}
is the frame-synchronous concatenation of the four on-board modality groups
detailed in Section~\ref{sec:tera_gen}: behavioral-visual channels from the
cabin-facing detector, kinematic channels (speed, longitudinal accelerations,
gyroscope), driving-context channels (road type, precipitation, illumination,
traffic density), and acoustic event channels, plus two detector-reliability
indicators -- yielding $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^{31}$ per frame at
$\approx$9.6\,fps. At each timestep the \mbox{MHEG} gate inspects only the
kinematic variation $\Delta x_t$ and the buffered entropy $H(p_{t-1})$; the
full vector reaches the recurrent core only when the gate fires.}
The claim that trajectory organization is what lets \MHEG schedule safely is tested,
not assumed: it is assessed through entropic separation, coverage stability, allocator selectivity, and $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ across the factorial configurations.
\AFTOI supplies the organization offline; the negative control removes it, so its
role shows up as the difference between the two.
Figure~\ref{fig:tera_operational} summarizes the operational architecture. The framework separates offline induction from online inference. Offline, \TERAGen, the non-causal reference model, \AFTOI, entropy stratification, and threshold calibration produce an organized causal model and calibrated thresholds. Online, the deployed causal model and \MHEG use this artifact to perform selective computation under coverage-preservation constraints.

\paragraph{Operational problem formulation}
Table~\ref{tab:objetivos_expandida} lists six design objectives, of which one
arbitrates the central conflict: $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$. Traditional
latency metrics such as $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{det}}$ are computed only over
detected episodes and ignore complete misses, which can make a system that
misses a fraction of events report a low average latency on the rest. A
scheduler may thus reduce apparent cost without changing the miss rate, and a
coverage-blind metric would not notice. The effective detection delay
integrates timeliness and coverage,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}
=
\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{det}}
+
\mathrm{FR}\times T_{\mathrm{ep}}
\label{eq:ttdef_sys}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{det}}$ is the mean latency over detected episodes,
$\mathrm{FR}$ is the missed-episode proportion, and $T_{\mathrm{ep}}$ is episode
duration. TERA can be read as a trustworthy selective computation mechanism for
safety-critical vehicular edge environments, with operational formulation
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min \quad &
\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}} \\
\text{subject to} \quad &
\mathrm{FR} \le 0.05
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:tera_operational_problem}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:tera_operational_problem} abstracts the objectives that guide
design and evaluation; it is not an optimization problem solved explicitly.
Among configurations satisfying $\mathrm{FR}\le0.05$, selective computation is
not an independent objective but a consequence of the scheduler identifying
redundant regions without compromising critical-event detection. The six
objectives are auxiliary detection (Balanced F1~\cite{chinchor1992muc}),
auxiliary calibration (ECE~\cite{guo2017calibration}), auxiliary partial
timeliness (gross delay $\mathrm{TTD}_b$~\cite{fawcett1999activity}), the
primary effective timeliness $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:ttdef_sys}),
the safety constraint $\mathrm{FR}\le0.05$~\cite{iso26262}, and auxiliary
computational economy (selective suppression rate,
\%Sup~\cite{campos2018skip}).

\subsection{Deterministic Temporal Anchoring (\mbox{TERA-Gen})}
\label{sec:tera_gen}
Controlled induction and frame-precise evaluation presuppose a deterministic
onset as reference. In naturalistic datasets, onset is fixed by retrospective
annotation with variability of $\approx$0.31\,s at 9.6\,fps, which masks real
operational differences between schedulers by making it impossible to tell
whether the allocator invested resources at emergence windows. \TERAGen is a
controlled laboratory for this, not a substitute for naturalistic data. By
anchoring risk targets to SHRP2 odds ratios it preserves epidemiological
coherence while removing the variability that would obscure the effect of
entropic differentiation on selectivity.
Scenarios span progressive and abrupt risk transitions, enabling frame-precise
quantification of $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$, miss rate, and robustness. The
system models 31 attributes across visual, kinematic, contextual, and acoustic
domains grounded in traffic epidemiology.
\paragraph{Input vector structure}
The vector $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^{31}$ aggregates four domains: behavioral visual
(seven driving classes, lateral and gaze deviation), kinematic (speed,
longitudinal accelerations, gyroscopic signals), contextual (road type,
precipitation, night driving, traffic density), and acoustic (siren, horn,
phone ring, background noise). Two further indicators encode instantaneous
detector reliability.
\paragraph{Epidemiological calibration}
Risk targets are projected onto $[0.05, 1.00]$ by
\begin{equation}
  \textit{risk\_target} \approx 0.05 + \frac{\mathrm{OR} - 1}{12.5}
  \label{eq:or_mapping}
\end{equation}
which preserves ordinal severity without claiming to model absolute collision
probability. Table~\ref{tab:shrp2_calibracao} gives the resulting values, and
the continuous reference label combines mean trajectory and peak intensity,
\begin{equation}
  y_{\text{ep}} = 0.7\,\mathrm{mean}(y_{\mathrm{frame}}) +
  0.3\,p_{95}(y_{\mathrm{frame}})
  \label{eq:agregacao}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Emergency regimes as design principle}
\TERAGen builds critical episodes in two ramp patterns. The \textit{progressive}
regime (60\% of critical scenarios) simulates gradual development with a latent
baseline of 8--20 timesteps and a ramp of $\approx$1.4\,s. The \textit{abrupt}
regime (the remaining 40\%) models fast-ramp perturbations $<$0.6\,s, the worst
case for coverage under adaptive computation, so the scheduler is tested exactly
where it must allocate resources to a rapidly emerging risk.
\paragraph{Methodological choice of binary supervision}
Episodes use binary supervision (Normal vs.\ Critical). Section~\ref{sec:mov6_emergente}
verifies that this onset-anchored supervision suffices to induce continuous
ordinal probabilistic trajectories. Continuous SHRP2-calibrated targets generate
temporally structured trajectories; training labels classify episodes as Normal
or Critical, isolating temporal structuring from refined risk regression.
Deterministic onset is the precondition for induction, because aligning causal trajectories to a structural signal is only meaningful when the onset instant is fixed and variability-free. \AFTOI operates directly on this anchor.
\subsection{Temporal Organization Induction (\mbox{AF-TOI})}
\label{sec:aftoi}
\AFTOI induces temporal organization rather than transferring accuracy. It uses
onset-oriented reference-to-target structural alignment during offline training,
aiming at predictive dynamics that separate stable regions, pre-onset regions,
and transition windows, which is distinct from model compression. Once that
differentiation emerges, predictive uncertainty stops behaving as a generic
confidence estimate and becomes structured by the temporal phase of the episode.
This organization is the enabling property studied here. As trajectories acquire
temporal structure, entropy stratifies across episode phases, separating routine
intervals from critical transitions, and that stratification is the scheduling
signal \MHEG depends on. Resource allocation remains a downstream consequence of
the induced structure, not a direct output of \AFTOI.
The causal constraint of embedded inference motivates the induction: a
unidirectional LSTM sees only accumulated history, so abrupt transitions with
sub-0.6\,s pre-onset windows risk weakly differentiated uncertainty and
insufficient stratification.
To overcome this, \AFTOI uses a non-causal BiLSTM reference with bidirectional
access to each episode's full temporal evolution. Free of causal restrictions,
the reference develops trajectories that encode the organization of risk
transitions across the whole window. \AFTOI transfers this structure to the
causal target through offline alignment, so the target reproduces temporally
organized dynamics while staying causal at deployment. The induction stays in
the development environment, and only the resulting causal model is exported.
\paragraph{Onset-Oriented Temporal Loss Function}
The causal target is optimized under the composite loss
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{AF\text{-}TOI}}$, balancing supervised anchoring with
alignment to the reference structural signal,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{AF\text{-}TOI}} = (1-\lambda_{\mathrm{TOI}})
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{hard}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{TOI}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{soft}}
  \label{eq:perda_total_aftoi}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\mathrm{TOI}} \in [0,1]$ balances supervision and alignment. The
supervised term is binary cross-entropy,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{hard}} = - \left[ y_{\text{ep}}\log(\hat{p}_{\text{ep}})
    + (1 - y_{\text{ep}}) \log(1 - \hat{p}_{\text{ep}}) \right]
  \label{eq:perda_hard_aftoi}
\end{equation}
Structural alignment is modeled by KL divergence with temperature $T=2.0$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{soft}}(t) = T^2 \cdot \mathrm{KL}\!\left(
    \sigma\!\bigl(\tfrac{z_r(t)}{T}\bigr) \,\Big\|\,
    \sigma\!\bigl(\tfrac{z_s(t)}{T}\bigr) \right)
  \label{eq:perda_soft_aftoi}
\end{equation}
with $z_r(t)$ the reference logits and $z_s(t)$ the causal-target logits at
timestep $t$. Asymmetric temporal weighting $w(t)$ concentrates induction at
onset regions,
\begin{equation}
w(t)= \begin{cases}
w_{\text{pre}}, & \max(0,\,t_0-k) \le t < t_0 \\[4pt]
\exp\!\bigl(-\alpha (t-t_0)\bigr), & t \ge t_0 \\[4pt]
1, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:ponderacao_aftoi}
\end{equation}
This weighting depends on the \TERAGen deterministic reference, evidencing the interdependency between ecosystem components.
\paragraph{Three-Phase Structural Alignment Scheduler}
Direct coupling to the reference signal in early epochs can cause gradient
instability, so $\lambda_{\mathrm{TOI}}(e)$ is regulated incrementally,
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{\mathrm{TOI}}(e)=
\begin{cases}
0, & 1 \leq e \leq E_{\text{warm}} \\[6pt]
\displaystyle\frac{e - E_{\text{warm}}}{E_{\text{ramp}} - E_{\text{warm}}},
  & E_{\text{warm}} < e \leq E_{\text{ramp}} \\[10pt]
1.0, & E_{\text{ramp}} < e \leq E_{\text{total}}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:scheduler_aftoi}
\end{equation}
with $E_{\text{warm}}=10$, $E_{\text{ramp}}=17$, $E_{\text{total}}=80$ epochs.
The scheduler is a \textbf{constitutive} part of \AFTOI; removing it compromises
coupling stability and the quality of the induced differentiation, as the
architectural ablation confirms (Section~\ref{sec:ablacao_teacher}).
The resulting stratification, with stable intervals at low entropy and pre-onset and transition windows at elevated, varying uncertainty, is the scheduling signal exploited next.
\subsection{Trustworthy Selective Computation (\mbox{MHEG})}
\label{sec:mheg}
\MHEG reads the stratification that \AFTOI produces; it does not create it. That
dependence is deliberate and consequential: the scheduler's usefulness is inherited
from the offline-induced organization, not from the gating rule on its own. When the stratification is absent, the gate has no basis for discrimination, which is precisely the failure reproduced by the negative control.
\MHEG is the runtime scheduler and adaptive inference controller of the embedded
system. At each timestep it decides whether to run a full LSTM update or reuse
the previous state, using the \AFTOI-induced organization. Its logic rests on
two components, synthesized in Figure~\ref{fig:mheg_componentes}.
\begin{figure}[!tbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{mheg_componentes.png}
\caption{\mbox{MHEG} runtime computation scheduler structure. The kinematic
component is an unconditional resource allocation trigger that forces
LSTM computation whenever physical state changes abruptly, regardless of
entropic state. The entropic component is the trajectory-dependent
scheduling signal. When temporal organization is absent, entropy remains
undifferentiated and the scheduler loses temporal discrimination power;
when induced by \mbox{AF-TOI}, entropy stratifies near onset and the scheduler
can allocate resources to emergence windows while safely suppressing
redundant intervals.}
\label{fig:mheg_componentes}
\end{figure}
\paragraph{Kinematic Component as Unconditional Resource Allocation Trigger}
\begin{equation}
  |\Delta x_t| = \max_{k \in \mathcal{K}} \bigl|x_t^{(k)} - x_{t-1}^{(k)}\bigr|
  \label{eq:delta_kin}
\end{equation}
When this variation exceeds $\tau_\Delta$, the LSTM core runs regardless of
entropic state. The unconditional trigger guarantees allocation in abrupt
episodes with sub-0.6\,s pre-onset windows, where entropic differentiation may
not manifest before the transition begins.
\paragraph{Entropic Component as Trajectory-Dependent Scheduling Signal}
\begin{equation}
H(p_{t-1}) = -p_{t-1}\log_2 p_{t-1} - (1 - p_{t-1})\log_2(1 - p_{t-1})
\label{eq:entropia}
\end{equation}
When entropy exceeds $\tau_H$, the scheduler invests full LSTM processing. Here
entropy is a decision variable reflecting trajectory instability, not an error
indicator. The scheduler does not need globally calibrated confidence; it needs
uncertainty with temporal differentiation. Absent global calibration does not
compromise allocation, but absent temporal differentiation does.
The discriminative power of the entropic component depends on trajectory
organization. Without \AFTOI-induced differentiation, entropy sits in a narrow
range and the scheduler loses selectivity; with it, phases stratify along the
entropic axis and the criterion gains temporal discrimination. This dependency
is the causal relationship at the center of the hypothesis, verified in
Section~\ref{sec:avaliacao}.
\paragraph{Joint Threshold Calibration}
With causal-target weights frozen, a per-seed grid search over the validation
set selects the threshold combination that maximizes suppression while keeping
$\mathrm{FR} \le 0.05$. Calibration uses \TERAGen synthetic data; online
re-calibration is a necessary extension for production.
\paragraph{Scheduling Criterion via Logical Disjunction}
\begin{equation}
\max_{k \in \mathcal{K}} \bigl|x_t^{(k)} - x_{t-1}^{(k)}\bigr| \ge \tau_\Delta
\quad \boldsymbol{\lor} \quad H(p_{t-1}) \ge \tau_H
\label{eq:gating}
\end{equation}
When neither condition holds, the LSTM update is suppressed and previous values
propagate. The disjunction reflects the asymmetric cost of safety-critical edge
systems, where suppressing computation at a transition window is far costlier
than a redundant inference step.
\paragraph{Operational Regimes}
Threshold magnitude determines three regimes. As thresholds $\to 0$, the system
runs full continuous inference: nothing is suppressed, cost is maximal, FR is
zero. As thresholds $\to \infty$, suppression is total and equivalent to halting
all LSTM updates: cost is zero, FR is maximal. Between these, per-seed
calibration on validation data yields the trustworthy hybrid regime the
ecosystem occupies, maximizing economy under $\mathrm{FR}\le0.05$. The next
subsection describes online execution.
\subsection{Runtime Inference and Risk Assessment}
\label{sec:runtime}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:inferencia} formalizes runtime execution at the edge device. The continuous probability $p_t$ is mapped to four discrete risk
levels.
\begin{equation}
  \ell_t = \begin{cases}
    0 \;\; \text{Normal},    & p_t < 0.15 \\
    1 \;\; \text{Attention}, & 0.15 \le p_t < 0.38 \\
    2 \;\; \text{Alert},     & 0.38 \le p_t < 0.50 \\
    3 \;\; \text{Critical},  & p_t \ge 0.50
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:alerta}
\end{equation}
\begin{algorithm}[!tbp]
\caption{Efficient inference cycle of the \mbox{MHEG} runtime computation scheduler}
\label{alg:inferencia}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Weights $\theta_{\text{LSTM}}$; thresholds $\tau_\Delta$,
  $\tau_H$, $\theta_1{=}0.15$, $\theta_2{=}0.38$, $\theta_3{=}0.50$;
  $h_0 {=} \mathbf{0}$;\; $p_0 {=} 0$
\ENSURE  Level $\ell_t \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ emitted at each timestep $t$
\FOR{each frame $x_t$}
    \STATE $\Delta x_t \leftarrow \max_{k \in \mathcal{K}} |x_t^{(k)} -
      x_{t-1}^{(k)}|$
    \STATE $H_t \leftarrow H(p_{t-1})$
    \IF{$(\Delta x_t \geq \tau_\Delta)$ \OR $(H_t \geq \tau_H)$}
        \STATE $(p_t, h_t) \leftarrow \mathrm{LSTM}(x_t, h_{t-1};\,
          \theta_{\text{LSTM}})$ \hfill \textit{[Invest computation]}
    \ELSE
        \STATE $p_t \leftarrow p_{t-1}$;\; $h_t \leftarrow h_{t-1}$
          \hfill \textit{[Reuse buffer -- suppress computation]}
    \ENDIF
    \STATE $\ell_t \leftarrow \mathbf{1}[p_t \ge \theta_3]\cdot 3
      + \mathbf{1}[\theta_2 \le p_t < \theta_3]\cdot 2
      + \mathbf{1}[\theta_1 \le p_t < \theta_2]\cdot 1$
    \STATE \textbf{Emit} $\ell_t$
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
{\color{\revcolor}The per-timestep complexity of Algorithm~\ref{alg:inferencia} is
dominated by line~5. Lines~2--4 and 8--10 cost $\mathcal{O}(D)$ and
$\mathcal{O}(1)$, respectively (a maximum over $|\mathcal{K}| \le D$ channel
differences, one closed-form binary entropy, and three threshold comparisons),
and involve no matrix operation. The recurrent update of line~5 costs
$\mathcal{O}(4(DH{+}H^2))$ and executes only when the disjunction of line~4
fires, so the expected per-frame cost is
$c_{\mathrm{gate}} + (1-s)\,\mathcal{O}(4(DH{+}H^2))$, where $s$ is the
suppression rate (\%Sup) and $c_{\mathrm{gate}}$ the constant gate overhead.
The worst case (no suppression) equals the full LSTM cost plus
$\mathcal{O}(D)$; the best case is the gate alone.}

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:avaliacao}

Validation follows six movements that track the hypothesis, each answering one
question and preparing the next. Movements~1 and~2 examine the mechanism:
whether \AFTOI observably induces temporal organization in the causal target,

\paragraph{Threshold configuration and calibration}
Thresholds $\tau_\Delta$ and $\tau_H$ are calibrated per seed via grid search
on the validation partition, selecting the configuration that maximizes
suppression under $\mathrm{FR}\le0.05$. A detection is valid only when
$p_t \ge \theta_{\mathrm{TTD}}$ for $m=3$ consecutive timesteps.
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{det}} = (t_{\mathrm{det}} - t_0)\cdot\Delta t,
\;\;
t_{\mathrm{det}} = \min\{t \ge t_0 \mid p_t, p_{t+1}, p_{t+2} \ge
\theta_{\mathrm{TTD}}\}
\label{eq:ttd_nominal}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}} =
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{det}}, & \text{if } t_{\mathrm{det}}
  \text{ exists} \\
T_{\mathrm{ep}} = 10.0\,\text{s}, & \text{otherwise (missed episode)}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:ttd_efetivo}
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Infrastructure and significance tests}
Latency is measured in unit-batch mode ($N_{\text{batch}}=1$) on an
NVIDIA RTX~3050~Ti GPU with PyTorch~2.0 and 100-iteration warmup.
Hypotheses use the paired Wilcoxon test~\cite{wilcoxon1945}, with effect
quantified by Cliff's delta~\cite{cliff1993dominance} and 95\%
confidence intervals by recipe-stratified
bootstrap~\cite{efron1993introduction}.
\subsection{Movement 1. Temporal Mechanism}
\label{sec:mov1_mecanismo}
Movement~1 checks, at frame level, whether \AFTOI observably reorganizes the
causal target's internal trajectory structure and thereby gives \MHEG a
temporally discriminative signal. This mechanistic evidence precedes any
attribution of allocation gains to organization.
In the Baseline, $H_t$ stays poorly differentiated across driving phases: phase
distributions collapse into a narrow homogeneous range with no visible
separation between stable and transition zones
(Figure~\ref{fig:entropy_dist}(a)). The scheduling signal is then
undiscriminating at the periods of highest criticality, with no temporal basis
to distinguish emergence windows from redundant intervals. With \AFTOI, phases
stratify along the entropic axis (Figure~\ref{fig:entropy_dist}(b)): Normal
anchors near zero, Pre-onset converges to the central region
($\tfrac{\ln 2}{2}$), and Post-onset accumulates at the maximum ($\ln 2$). This
is the pattern that gives \MHEG a usable scheduling signal.
As a byproduct of the induction, alignment between declared confidence and
empirical frame-level accuracy improves, with
$\text{ECE}_{\mathrm{frame}} = 0.044$ for \AFTOI versus $0.129$ for Baseline
(Figure~\ref{fig:entropy_dist}(c)). This calibration gain accompanies
stratification but is not the objective.

\label{sec:mov2_seletividade}

\paragraph{The negative control as unsafe adaptive computation}
Applying \MHEG to unorganized trajectories has non-neutral effects on allocation
quality. Baseline+MHEG holds $\text{FR} = \resBaseHibrFail \pm \stdBaseHibrFail$
at the fixed-baseline level, so it does not reduce misses, and it still degrades
$\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ by {\color{\revcolor}13.9\%}, from $\resBaseFixoTTDef$\,s to
$\resBaseHibrTTDef$\,s. The scheduler allocates without temporal discrimination,
suppressing emerging-risk timesteps as often as redundant ones. This is the
unsafe regime: apparent per-frame cost drops with no coverage guarantee while
the effective delay worsens. The result points to the absence of the enabling
property, not a scheduler design flaw.
\paragraph{The complete ecosystem and trustworthy selective computation}
Under \AFTOI organization, \MHEG operates on differentiated trajectories. The
miss rate is preserved at $\resAlvoHibrFail \pm \stdAlvoHibrFail$, with
$\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ rising only {\color{\revcolor}0.016}\,s relative to \AFTOI fixed. The
latency impact of scheduling is about {\color{\revcolor}4.3}$\times$ smaller in the complete
ecosystem than in the negative control {\color{\revcolor}(0.016\,s vs.\ 0.070\,s)}, and absolute
$\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ with active scheduling is {\color{\revcolor}0.099\,s versus 0.570\,s}.
In this synthetic scenario, that convergence is consistent with efficiency and
coverage coexisting once the scheduler has temporally organized trajectories.
\paragraph{Regime asymmetry in progressive episodes}
\AFTOI Fixed shows {\color{\revcolor}$\text{TTD}_p = 1.200 \pm 0.437$}\,s in progressive episodes,
above the Baseline {\color{\revcolor}($0.657 \pm 0.325$\,s)}: reorganizing dynamics at transition
regions can delay classification consolidation in slowly developing episodes.
The benefit concentrates in the abrupt regime, where the miss rate falls from
{\color{\revcolor}0.082 to 0.004}, exactly where misses cost most.
\paragraph{TTD$_b$ vs.\ TTD$_{\mathrm{ef}}$}
Gross delay counts only detected episodes and reads optimistically because it
hides misses. $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ corrects this for edge systems where
coverage across all episodes matters more than mean latency on successful
detections.

\subsection{Epistemological Control via Post-hoc Calibration}
\label{sec:controle_calibracao}
A complementary question is whether temporal organization could be approximated
by post-hoc calibration of Baseline probabilities. Three techniques were
applied: temperature scaling (TS)~\cite{guo2017calibration}, Platt scaling (PS)~\cite{platt1999probabilistic},
and isotonic calibration (IC)~\cite{zadrozny2002isotonic}. \MHEG was applied to each variant with thresholds
re-calibrated per seed to maximize suppression under $\mathrm{FR} \le 0.05$,
yielding Table~\ref{tab:calibracao_posthoc}.
Probabilistic alignment improves in all variants. Uncalibrated Baseline ECE is
$\approx 0.035$; after isotonic calibration it drops to $\CalibICFixedECE$
(reduction $\CalibICFixedECEDELTA$). The techniques work as intended.
{\color{\revcolor}A note on scale: ECE values in this paper are
protocol-specific -- the factorial evaluation of
Table~\ref{tab:resultados_principais}, the frame-level reliability diagram of
Figure~\ref{fig:entropy_dist}, and the calibration study of this subsection
each compute ECE over a different probability stream, partition, and binning
-- so magnitudes are comparable within, but not across, these analyses.}
Their operational profiles under \MHEG differ sharply. Temperature scaling
degrades $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ from $\CalibTSFixedTTDef$\,s to
$\CalibTSHybrTTDef$\,s, reproducing the unsafe regime of the negative control.
Platt scaling and isotonic calibration reach lower $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$
under \MHEG ($\CalibPSHybrTTDef$\,s and $\CalibICHybrTTDef$\,s), which needs a
precise mechanistic reading to avoid misinterpretation.
The key property is that post-hoc calibration is a monotonic transformation of
output probabilities: it preserves the ordering of $p(t)$ along time and cannot
reorganize the temporal structure of $H(t)$.
Figure~\ref{fig:calibracao_entropia_temporal} confirms this. In panel~(a), TS,
PS, and IC preserve the Baseline temporal morphology, and the entropy profile
around onset stays undifferentiated. The $H_{\mathrm{strat}}$ scores match the
Baseline across seeds ($\CalibTSFixedHStrat \pm \CalibTSFixedHStratStd$ for TS,
$\CalibICFixedHStrat \pm \CalibICFixedHStratStd$ for IC), so no technique
reorganizes temporal entropy dynamics. In panel~(b), \AFTOI structurally shifts
the trajectory, elevating $H(t)$ near onset and suppressing it in stable phases.
The lower $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ for PS and IC under \MHEG does not come
from temporal reorganization. Both redistribute the probability scale, and IC in
particular compresses probabilities toward empirical frequencies, shifting the
operating regime of the entropy-based gate without altering the temporal
ordering of uncertainty. When \MHEG thresholds are re-calibrated to that scale,
lower suppression cost is achievable at a given coverage. This is a threshold
calibration effect, not an organizational one: $H_{\mathrm{strat}}$ stays within
the Baseline range and the operational frontier in
Figure~\ref{fig:curva_operacional} does not move to a qualitatively distinct
location. \AFTOI, by contrast, induces an organization regime not reproduced by
probability-scale adjustment, with $H_{\mathrm{strat}}$ values that
systematically exceed the calibrated Baseline range across seeds.

\subsection{Confidence-Threshold and Smoothed-Entropy Baselines}
\label{sec:gating_baselines}
Two additional scheduling baselines complete the comparison requested by the
selective-inference literature: a \emph{confidence-threshold gate}, which
updates only when the buffered confidence is low (equivalently, when
$H(p_{t-1}) \ge \tau$), and a \emph{smoothed-entropy gate}, which thresholds a
moving average of the buffered entropy (window $w{=}5$). Both use no kinematic
channel. They are simulated under a replay protocol on the stored
full-inference trajectories of the factorial study (seeds 42--45): on update,
the buffered probability is refreshed from the stored trajectory; on
suppression, it is held. Thresholds are oracle-calibrated on the test
partition (maximum suppression subject to $\mathrm{FR} \le 0.05$, jointly with
the detection threshold), an upper bound that \emph{favors} the baselines.
Magnitudes are on the replay scale and comparable within
Table~\ref{tab:gating_baselines} only.

Table~\ref{tab:gating_baselines} reports two operating points. At the safe
point, the purely entropic gates collapse: without an external wake-up signal,
a buffered low-entropy value never fires again (the gate deadlocks), so any
non-negligible suppression forfeits coverage, and the maximum safe suppression
is $0$--$2\%$ on either trajectory type. Temporal smoothing does not repair
this, since averaging a frozen buffer changes nothing. At the
matched-suppression point (tuned to spend the same fraction of frames as the
entropy+kinematic gate), the confidence and smoothed gates miss
$99\%$ of critical episodes on unorganized trajectories and $59\%$ on
organized ones. The entropy+kinematic gate, in contrast, sustains
$\mathrm{FR}=0$ at $96\%$ and $69\%$ suppression, and the benefit of temporal
organization appears in its effective delay: $0.96$\,s on \mbox{AF-TOI}
trajectories against $1.90$\,s on unorganized ones at comparable safety. This
supports two claims at once: the unconditional kinematic trigger is a
structural necessity for any buffered entropy gate (Section~3.3), and
organization, not the gating rule, is what converts suppression into safe
suppression.

\paragraph{Experimental evaluation synthesis}
Taken together, the six movements converge on a single conclusion: in this embedded causal setting, temporal differentiation is what enables coverage-preserving selective inference, with driver monitoring as the concrete case. \AFTOI induces the differentiation that
makes \MHEG's criterion discriminative, so computation goes to emergence windows and

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussao}
%%============================================================
\paragraph{Temporal organization as the enabling condition for edge resource allocation}
The most informative result is the negative control, rather than the full configuration. Baseline+MHEG holds the miss rate but buys no efficiency in return,
which locates \AFTOI's primary effect precisely: it is not that \AFTOI raises
classification scores, but that it creates the conditions under which selective
computation is safe at all. Without differentiated dynamics, efficiency and
coverage do not merely fail to improve together -- they compete, because the
scheduler trims apparent per-frame cost while the effective delay climbs. Once the
trajectories are organized, that competition relaxes
(Movements~\ref{sec:mov1_mecanismo} and~\ref{sec:mov2_seletividade}): $H_t$ turns
into a variable the scheduler can discriminate on, concentrating computation at
high-instability timesteps and reusing buffers through the stable ones. The premise
behind SkipRNN~\cite{campos2018skip} and ACT~\cite{graves2016adaptive} -- that low
uncertainty marks content safe to skip -- holds in activity recognition and breaks
in causal edge inference, and the negative control is where that break becomes
measurable. In summary, the effectiveness of the runtime allocator depends on a property the model must already possess: temporal differentiation in its trajectories.
\paragraph{AF-TOI as enabler of trustworthy selective computation}
A model with 95\% classification accuracy and an unsafe scheduler is not
equivalent to one whose scheduler allocates correctly at emergence windows. TERA
shows that scheduler quality is at least as important as classification quality,
and that protocols optimizing only accuracy or calibration do not guarantee the
trajectory structure scheduling reliability depends on, a direct implication for
edge-native ITS design.
\paragraph{Probabilistic calibration and temporal organization as
independent edge design properties}
The post-hoc calibration control carries a direct design implication.
Temperature, Platt, and isotonic scaling improve aggregate alignment without
touching weights or internal trajectories, and the $H(t)$ morphology around
onset stays within the Baseline range for all three, confirming that monotonic
rescaling does not induce temporal differentiation. Where PS and IC reach lower
$\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ under \MHEG, it is threshold recalibration to the
redistributed scale, not organizational restructuring, as the invariant
$H_{\mathrm{strat}}$ profile shows. The frontier reachable through temporal
induction stays qualitatively distinct from what scale adjustment can reach.
Marginal calibration and temporally conditioned operational reliability vary
independently and have distinct mechanistic origins.
\paragraph{Effective coverage versus gross delay}
The Baseline shows lower gross delay on detected episodes, but that metric omits
misses. For safety-critical edge systems, coverage across all episodes matters
more than mean latency on successes, and any metric excluding misses reads
optimistically.
\paragraph{Complementary role of the kinematic component}
When pre-onset windows fall below 0.6\,s, temporal differentiation may not
appear in entropy fast enough. \MHEG's kinematic component covers this by
triggering allocation unconditionally when instantaneous physical variation
signals a sudden state change. The disjunction between components handles two
emergency regimes with distinct dynamics without depending exclusively on
entropy, which matters for robustness under abrupt onset.
\paragraph{Implications for edge AI deployment and vehicular edge integration}
Most deployment properties follow from the offline/online separation. The computationally intensive stages -- \TERAGen, the BiLSTM reference, \AFTOI, and threshold calibration -- remain in the development environment; the device runs only the causal LSTM, the
calibrated thresholds, and the recurrent buffer, with no reference model to execute
and no on-line retraining. \MHEG's own overhead is $O(1)$ per timestep, negligible
beside the recurrent update it gates. At $\approx$58\,K parameters, a 1,024-byte
buffer, and $\approx$28\,KFLOPs per timestep, the deployed model sits within the
envelope of current automotive-grade microcontrollers and accelerators, and because
the only artifacts that move to the device are calibrated weights and thresholds, it
is compatible with OTA updates and needs no connectivity at inference time. The
$\ell_t$ signal, produced at $O(1)$ cost, is the natural point of contact with
higher-level VEC and MEC coordination -- but that contact is an implication we
develop in Section~\ref{sec:vecmec}, not something evaluated here.
\paragraph{Future directions}
Three extensions follow. First, the \MHEG scheduling signal could feed future VEC
and MEC offloading policies (Section~\ref{sec:vecmec}), triggering remote
inference when local entropy exceeds a coordination threshold while suppressing
redundant timesteps and reallocating the saved energy. Second, online adaptation
of \MHEG thresholds under distributional shift is the principal step toward
production. Third, adversarial robustness under sensor faults, malicious
perturbations, and modality degradation is a natural safety extension.
\paragraph{Scope and applicability}
\label{sec:scope}
The campaign is scoped to a single embedded node running continuous causal
inference, which isolates the temporal-organization effect from network-level
confounders. No networked coordination is evaluated. The mechanism could be
reused in future distributed deployments; Section~\ref{sec:vecmec} discusses how
the \MHEG entropy gate could inform VEC and MEC offloading, with empirical
validation under real network conditions left as future work. Robustness against
adversarial attacks, sensor faults, and cybersecurity threats is out of scope
and noted in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
\paragraph{Methodological rationale for synthetic evaluation}
The systematic use of \TERAGen is deliberate. Controlled evaluation of temporal
organization needs three properties naturalistic datasets cannot provide
together at the required granularity: (i) \emph{deterministic onset annotation},
removing the $\approx$0.31\,s variability that would make scheduler differences
indistinguishable at 9.6\,fps; (ii) \emph{causal isolation}, preventing
correlated naturalistic confounders from masking the effect of trajectory
structure on selectivity; and (iii) \emph{frame-precise attribution}, enabling
exact quantification of when the scheduler allocates relative to onset. \TERAGen
anchors synthetic trajectories to SHRP2 odds ratios~\cite{dingus2016shrp2},
preserving statistical realism while achieving annotation precision naturalistic
collection cannot guarantee. It does not replace naturalistic evaluation; it
makes the hypothesis testable at the required granularity. Generalization to
naturalistic data is the primary direction for future validation (Limitation~1).
\paragraph{Threats to validity and deployment limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Four limitations bound the claims. First, evaluation relies on \TERAGen
synthetic data, designed to isolate the causal relationship between temporal
organization and trustworthy selective computation under controlled onset;
confirmation on naturalistic datasets such as DMD~\cite{ortega2020dmd} and
DADA~\cite{fang2022dada} remains necessary for external validity. Second, \MHEG
thresholds are calibrated on synthetic data and may need re-calibration under
real-world shift from driver variability, illumination, and sensor noise.
{\color{\revcolor}Third, the factorial analysis now uses twelve seeds (42--53),
expanded in this revision from the original four under an a priori protocol
(seeds fixed before any $p$-value; all reported); with $n=12$ the exact paired
Wilcoxon test reaches conventional significance on F1, ECE, and
$\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:mov3_consequencia}). The
remaining statistical caveat is narrower: FR in the fixed contrast is at a
ceiling (most seeds have zero misses in both configurations), and inter-seed
replication under a single synthetic generator quantifies sensitivity to
initialization, not external validity -- significance here does not substitute
for naturalistic validation. The mechanistic anchor remains the negative
control, where \MHEG on unorganized trajectories worsens
$\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ while the same scheduler on organized
trajectories preserves coverage and cuts computation.} Fourth, deployment feasibility is assessed through profiling and
analytical estimation rather than execution on physical automotive hardware, so
latency, energy, thermal behavior, and sensor-noise robustness remain to be
characterized on STM32H7-, Cortex-M55-, and SA8155P-class platforms.
{\color{\revcolor}A related caveat is that a controlled environment could, in
principle, favor an onset-oriented training strategy. Three design elements
mitigate, without eliminating, this risk: episodes mix progressive and abrupt
regimes in fixed proportion (Section~\ref{sec:tera_gen}); Movement~6 evaluates
recipe families never seen in training; and the stress campaign of
Section~\ref{sec:robustness_controls} spans nine onset/noise scenarios,
including degraded entropy, lagged entropy, missing kinematic channels, and
false onsets. Generalization across risk-trajectory families beyond these, and
under real distribution shift, remains to be established on naturalistic
data.}
\paragraph{Planned real-data validation}
As the primary next step we will test whether the temporal organization induced
on \TERAGen transfers to real driving video, using the
DADA2000 dataset~\cite{fang2022dada}, which provides frame-level accident
annotations -- an abnormal-onset frame, an accident frame, and an abnormal-end
frame -- together with annotated contextual attributes (weather, lighting, scene,
and road geometry). The abnormal-onset frame serves as a real, frame-precise
onset $t_0$, and the interval up to the accident frame is the emergence window in
which detection must occur; this is the property naturalistic driver datasets
usually lack and that \TERAGen was built to supply synthetically. The scene and
contextual attributes map onto the visual and contextual channels of our feature
schema. Because DADA2000 is scene-facing and carries no ego-vehicle kinematics,
the kinematic trigger of \MHEG has no native input; we will therefore evaluate the
entropic gate both on its own and with an optical-flow-based kinematic proxy, an
ablation that isolates the contribution of temporal organization from that of the
kinematic trigger. Evaluation will use clip-level splits stratified by scene and
accident type, with clips from the same source kept on the same side to prevent
leakage, and \MHEG thresholds re-calibrated per split on real validation data and
never transferred across domains. We will compare three transfer regimes --
zero-shot, with the organization-bearing recurrent layers frozen and only the
input projection adapted; light fine-tuning, with the recurrent layers frozen and
the input and output layers adapted; and full re-induction of \AFTOI on the real
training split -- against the same negative control used here (Baseline+MHEG).
Success is defined not by matching the synthetic magnitudes, which are on a
different scale, but by reproducing the qualitative relationships established on
\TERAGen: the negative control failing to improve coverage, and \mbox{AF-TOI}+MHEG
preserving coverage while suppressing computation. On the real data we will report
$\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$, FR, \%Sup, and near-onset false suppression
(FS@onset, Section~\ref{sec:robustness_controls}) with the dataset's own episode
length and sample size, alongside the accident-anticipation metrics standard for
that dataset. No real-data results are reported in this paper; this paragraph
states the planned protocol only, and the entropy-lag tolerance measured in
Section~\ref{sec:robustness_controls} sets the expectation that the scheduler
should absorb the timing imprecision of real annotations up to a bounded lag.
\section{Architectural Implications for Vehicular Edge Computing and Future Offloading}
\label{sec:vecmec}
This section offers an architectural interpretation, not an evaluated
distributed system. The campaign in Section~\ref{sec:avaliacao} ran on a single
embedded node under a fixed budget: no networked coordination, offloading,
communication latency, or bandwidth analysis was evaluated. The validated
contribution remains single-node embedded selective computation. Here we explain
only how the node-level operational state signal $\ell_t$, validated in that
setting, could inform future VEC and MEC architectures.
\subsection{TERA as a Source of Edge Operational State Signals}
\label{sec:tera_opsignal}
The \MHEG runtime output is a discrete signal
$\ell_t \in \{\text{Normal},\, \text{Attention},\, \text{Alert},\,
\text{Critical}\}$, emitted once per timestep at $O(1)$ cost
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:inferencia}, Eq.~\ref{eq:alerta}). It is compact, a two-bit
integer that can be stored, transmitted, or logged without reference to LSTM
weights, recurrent state, or sensor frames, and it is interpretable through the
epidemiological calibration of \TERAGen (Table~\ref{tab:shrp2_calibracao}). When
\MHEG suppresses the update, $\ell_t$ comes from the buffered $p_{t-1}$ with no
inference at all.
What sets $\ell_t$ apart from a thresholded classifier output is its temporal
grounding. Under \AFTOI, $\ell_t$ reflects phase-conditioned trajectory
structure rather than instantaneous confidence, and the negative control of
Section~\ref{sec:mov3_consequencia} shows a Baseline scheduler without this
structure degrades $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ by {\color{\revcolor}13.9\%}. This work's
contribution is producing $\ell_t$ with experimentally supported temporal
grounding; how it is consumed externally is a downstream question for future
work.
\subsection{Candidate Integration Paths for VEC and MEC Offloading}
\label{sec:vecmec_implications}
\paragraph{Resource allocation at the embedded tier}
The most immediate implication is within the on-board unit. Under \mbox{AF-TOI}+MHEG,
\SkipRate\% of frames are suppressed without violating $\mathrm{FR} \le 0.05$,
freeing cycles that could support other on-board functions. Whether such sharing
is feasible depends on the hardware platform and RTOS scheduler, which this work
does not evaluate.
\paragraph{Compatibility with VEC and MEC offloading}
In VEC and MEC architectures, the on-board unit decides which tasks to keep
local and which to offload to roadside or cloud infrastructure. The signal
$\ell_t$ could serve as one urgency input among others, with persistent Alert or
Critical indicating time-critical conditions and Normal or Attention indicating
deferrable ones. Such a policy could transmit only the compact symbol $\ell_t$
plus a summary feature window rather than raw frames, consistent with
bandwidth-constrained partial offloading~\cite{uddin2025taskoffloading}. The
activation fractions in Movement~6 (Section~\ref{sec:mov6_emergente}) are
episode-level severity indicators, not measured communication rates; translating
them into frame-level communication load requires network-level validation not
performed here.
\paragraph{From scheduling signal to coordination primitive.}
In the borderline setting of Movement~6, activation fraction was more
informative than instantaneous mean probability for summarizing the response to
intermediate-risk episodes. A future policy could escalate on the persistence of
elevated levels over a short window rather than on single readings, expressible
as a rolling escalation statistic,
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_t(w) = \frac{1}{w}\sum_{i=0}^{w-1} \mathbf{1}\!\left[\ell_{t-i}
  \ge 2\right],
  \label{eq:escalation}
\end{equation}
the fraction of the last $w$ timesteps at Alert or Critical. $\Phi_t(w)$ adds
only a bounded moving sum over a symbol the system already emits, at a memory
cost of $w$ two-bit symbols, negligible next to the 1{,}024-byte buffer. It is
not evaluated here but offered as a candidate bridge between the validated
single-node mechanism and a future multi-tier policy, consistent with
distributed early-exit designs~\cite{peng2025distree} and VEC offloading
frameworks~\cite{uddin2025taskoffloading,abouali2025edgeai}; a future system
could combine it with channel quality, queue length, and latency budget.
In summary, the single-node mechanism validated here is shaped, without
modification, as a candidate coordination primitive for future VEC and MEC
deployments, and the finding that activation frequency summarized escalating
risk more stably than instantaneous mean probability could inform how a future
escalation policy is designed. Empirical validation under real network
conditions remains future work.
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusao}
We set out to test whether selective computation can be made safe in strictly
causal vehicular and ad hoc edge inference, and to identify what the safety actually
depends on. Our hypothesis was that it depends less on the scheduler's policy than
on the temporal organization of the model's predictive trajectories -- the property
that makes uncertainty informative in the seconds around a critical transition.
Within the controlled setting studied here, the evidence supports that reading, and
the negative control is what makes it credible: with the scheduler applied to
unorganized trajectories, $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ worsened by
{\color{\revcolor}13.9\% (twelve seeds, $p=0.001$)} and
coverage did not improve, so activating a scheduler is not, by itself, enough. Given
\mbox{AF-TOI}-organized trajectories, the same \MHEG scheduler reached \CostReduction\%
effective cost reduction with $\mathrm{TTD}_{\mathrm{ef}}$ of
{\color{\revcolor}0.099\,s against 0.570\,s}. The post-hoc calibration control ruled out the competing explanation:
probabilistic rescaling improved ECE without reorganizing entropy in time, so
marginal calibration and temporally conditioned reliability are separate properties
with separate origins.
The design lesson is not specific to driver monitoring. A training protocol that
optimizes only classification accuracy or aggregate calibration can still leave a
model unsafe to schedule on, because neither objective constrains the internal
trajectory structure a causal scheduler reads. On this evidence, temporal
organization is worth treating as a design property in its own right for
resource-constrained edge AI, rather than as an accidental byproduct of training.
The pipeline ends in the operational-risk signal
$\ell_t \in \{\text{Normal},\, \text{Attention},\, \text{Alert},\, \text{Critical}\}$,
produced at $O(1)$ cost by the chain \emph{Temporal Organization $\rightarrow$
Entropy Stratification $\rightarrow$ MHEG}. Because it is compact, real-time, and
interpretable at the level of the trajectory (Section~\ref{sec:tera_opsignal}), it is
a plausible primitive for distributed and vehicular edge architectures to consume
without touching the on-board loop -- though we present that use as a design path
(Section~\ref{sec:vecmec}), not a validated result.
The scope of the present validation should be stated explicitly. TERA is validated
as a temporal-organization and scheduling substrate for a \emph{single} embedded
node, under strictly causal inference, on controlled synthetic data with a
deterministic onset; the whole deployed workflow fits in $\approx$58\,K parameters
and 1{,}024\,bytes of recurrent buffer, within reach of automotive-grade platforms.
It is not validated as a standalone detection system, and no networked coordination,
offloading, or multi-node behavior was evaluated. The VEC and MEC directions of
Section~\ref{sec:vecmec} are implications of the $\ell_t$ signal, to be tested rather
than claimed. Accordingly, the immediate priorities are external validity on
naturalistic data, robustness under distributional shift, sensor faults, and
adversarial perturbation, characterization on physical automotive hardware, and an
empirical multi-node study of the offloading policies sketched here -- each building
on the single-node, entropy-gated mechanism this paper establishes.
